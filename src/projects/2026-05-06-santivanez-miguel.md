# Historical Urban Evolution - Lima

Lima is the second driest biggest city in the world, after El Cairo. Through the Surco and extensive canal system, water transformed the desert into inhabitable and agricultural land, several centuries ago.

### From desert to agriculture fields
![Desert Valley](/projects/images/santivanez-miguel/1-desert-valley.jpg)
*Desert landscape of the valley.*

![Prehispanic Valley](/projects/images/santivanez-miguel/2-prehispanical-valley.jpg)
*Transformation into agricultural fields.*

In pre-Hispanic Lima, hydraulic infrastructure structured territorial authority. Control over water distribution through the development of this system over the centuries, local curacas such as **Taulichusco** held power over the territory.

### Prehispanic Evolution
![Early Intermediate](/projects/images/santivanez-miguel/600-early-intermediate.JPG)
![Middle Horizon](/projects/images/santivanez-miguel/1000-middle-horizon.JPG)
![Late Intermediate](/projects/images/santivanez-miguel/1460-late-intermediate.JPG)

The Surco Canal long predates the contemporary city of Lima. Constructed as part of a broader pre-Hispanic hydraulic network, the canal transformed an arid coastal territory into productive agricultural land.
